Bible verses for a job search, and for the work you already have

Seventeen verses for the months of applying with nothing coming back, for the work you still have to do meanwhile, and for the decision about which way to go. With a short prayer to write for each.

October 26, 20269 min read17 verses, World English Bible

A job search has a shape nothing else in adult life quite has. You do the work of applying, which is real work, and then nothing happens, and there is no way to tell from outside whether nothing means no or means not yet. Forty applications can produce four replies, three of them automated. The ones that go further ask you to describe yourself well for forty minutes, then go quiet for a fortnight.

The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible and grouped by where you actually are rather than by book. Notice what scripture spends its words on. There is very little here about getting hired and a great deal about working: how to do it, who you are doing it for, when to stop. Only the first group is really about the search.

Prayer is not a substitute for the dull practical work that moves a search: the message to someone who already works there, the honest look at whether the application was any good, the conversation about what you would take instead. If being out of work has gone on long enough that you are not sleeping or not wanting to be here, say that to a doctor as well as to God. Read the group that matches this week and leave the other two.

When you are applying and nothing comes back

This is the part people pray about and the part scripture handles least directly, which is worth knowing before you go looking. You will not find a verse about an inbox. What you will find is a set of promises about provision, guidance and being carried through a dry stretch.

Read them for their timing. Due season, in its season, continually. None names a date.

Let the favor of the Lord our God be on us. Establish the work of our hands for us. Yes, establish the work of our hands.

Psalm 90:17 · WEB

A request rather than a promise, which is why it is worth praying as written. The asking is for the work of our hands to last and to count, and the psalm says it twice as though once were not enough.

For I know the thoughts that I think toward you,” says the LORD, “thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you hope and a future.

Jeremiah 29:11 · WEB

Written to people in exile who had just been told to build houses in the city they did not want to live in, and that it would be seventy years. Hope and a future was true and was not quick.

Let’s not be weary in doing good, for we will reap in due season if we don’t give up.

Galatians 6:9 · WEB

Weary in doing good is the risk it names, which is the risk in month five of applying. Due season concedes a season and does not say which one you are in.

and the LORD will guide you continually, satisfy your soul in dry places, and make your bones strong. You will be like a watered garden, and like a spring of water whose waters don’t fail.

Isaiah 58:11 · WEB

What is promised is guidance and a soul satisfied in the dry place, not a quick exit from it. It arrives at the end of a passage about feeding hungry people, so the guidance comes attached to something to do.

The LORD will open to you his good treasure in the sky, to give the rain of your land in its season, and to bless all the work of your hand. You will lend to many nations, and you will not borrow.

Deuteronomy 28:12 · WEB

A covenant blessing to a nation, with conditions in the verses either side of it. The blessing lands on the work of your hand rather than instead of it.

I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.

Philippians 4:13 · WEB

Two sentences earlier the same writer says he has learned to be content both hungry and full. All things means the whole of that range, including the lean end.

A prayer to write

I have done the applying and I do not know whether the silence means no. Keep me from going bitter while I wait, and establish the work of my hands, whatever that turns out to be. If there is a door, show it to me plainly. If there is something I should be doing differently, let someone tell me.

When you still have to do the work in front of you

Most people reading a page like this are not only searching. There is a job to keep going to, or shifts that pay some of it, and the search runs underneath all of it. This is the stretch where the work you have turns into the thing you do while waiting for your real life to resume.

These verses are about doing work rather than finding it, and they are more interested in who you are working for than in whether the work is any good. One of them was written to people whose working conditions were not a choice at all.

And whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men,

Colossians 3:23 · WEB

Addressed in its first setting to household slaves, which should slow down anyone reading it as advice about engagement at work. Not for men is the part that does the work: it removes the audience you have been performing for.

Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might; for there is no work, nor plan, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in Sheol, where you are going.

Ecclesiastes 9:10 · WEB

The reason given for working hard is mortality, with nothing about reward attached. Whatever your hand finds to do takes the question of whether a task is beneath you off the table.

Do you see a man skilled in his work? He will serve kings. He won’t serve obscure men.

Proverbs 22:29 · WEB

A proverb about the practical value of craft, in the observational voice rather than the promising one. Becoming skilled takes longer than a job search.

and that you make it your ambition to lead a quiet life, and to do your own business, and to work with your own hands, even as we instructed you,

1 Thessalonians 4:11 · WEB

Ambition is the surprising word, and it is attached to a quiet life rather than to advancement. Written to people who had begun neglecting ordinary work because they expected the world to end shortly.

For you will eat the labor of your hands. You will be happy, and it will be well with you.

Psalm 128:2 · WEB

Happy and well are modest words, attached to eating what you worked for rather than to the scale of it. The psalm counts this a blessing rather than a baseline.

It is vain for you to rise up early, to stay up late, eating the bread of toil, for he gives sleep to his loved ones.

Psalm 127:2 · WEB

Not an argument against hard work but against the hours done out of fear, the early start and the late finish that fix nothing.

A prayer to write

This is not the work I wanted and it is the work I have. Let me do it properly today rather than resentfully, and for you rather than for anyone watching. Keep me from the late nights that are really fear. Let me eat what I earn and be glad of it while it lasts.

When you cannot tell which way to go

The third problem is direction. Two offers and no way to choose, or a field you are no longer sure you belong in, or a retraining course that costs a year you may not have spare. This is not a shortage of options but a shortage of certainty, and it keeps people awake longest.

The verses here use the same two verbs, commit and trust. None of them says the plan will be shown to you in advance. They describe handing something over and then being directed while moving.

Commit your deeds to the LORD, and your plans shall succeed.

Proverbs 16:3 · WEB

Commit is the verb to sit with, and the succeeding follows the handing over rather than preceding it. The proverb does not say your plan survives unchanged.

Commit your way to the LORD. Trust also in him, and he will do this:

Psalm 37:5 · WEB

The sentence ends on a colon in this translation and points at the verse after it. The psalm it sits in is an argument about not envying people whose careers are going better than yours.

Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and don’t lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight.

Proverbs 3:5-6 · WEB

Don't lean on your own understanding is the hard half for anyone told all their life to make a plan. Straight paths describes direction rather than ease, and in all your ways includes the ordinary working week.

A man’s heart plans his course, but the LORD directs his steps.

Proverbs 16:9 · WEB

Both halves are affirmed and neither cancels the other. You are expected to plan the course, and the steps are still directed as you take them.

A prayer to write

I cannot see far enough ahead to choose well. I am committing this to you, including the version of it I would prefer. Direct the steps I take this week, and make the next thing clear enough to act on even if the whole of it stays hidden.

Write down what you asked for, and date it

A job search is remembered badly, because the ending overwrites everything before it. Once you are in the new role, four months of silence compress into a sentence you tell people at parties, and the Tuesday you nearly gave up is gone. If it has not ended yet, the opposite happens: the whole stretch feels like one undifferentiated failure, and you lose track of how much you actually did.
