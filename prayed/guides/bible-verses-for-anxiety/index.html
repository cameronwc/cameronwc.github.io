Bible verses for anxiety, and how to actually pray them

Twelve verses for the racing-thoughts kind of night, grouped by what the anxiety is doing to you, with a short prayer to write for each.

September 11, 20266 min read12 verses, World English Bible

Anxiety does not usually arrive as one clear worry. It arrives as a hundred half-formed ones, all talking at once, most of them about things that have not happened. Praying through it is hard for the same reason: you cannot get a sentence out before the next thought interrupts.

The verses below are the ones people actually reach for. They are quoted word for word from the World English Bible, and they are grouped by what anxiety is doing to you right now, because the verse you need when you cannot sleep is not the verse you need when you are bracing for a phone call. Each group ends with a short prayer you could write down as it is, or change until it is yours.

One honest note first. These verses are not a technique, and they are not a substitute for a doctor when anxiety has become an illness. They are what people have prayed for a very long time when the thoughts would not stop, and they still work the way they always have: slowly, by being returned to.

When you need to hand it over

Most anxious prayer stalls at the first step, which is admitting the thing is too heavy. These are the verses that give you permission to set it down. Notice that none of them promise the problem goes away. They promise that you are not holding it alone.

casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.

1 Peter 5:7 · WEB

Half a sentence, and the whole argument is in the second half. The reason you can cast the worry is not that it is small. It is that he cares.

Cast your burden on the LORD and he will sustain you. He will never allow the righteous to be moved.

Psalm 55:22 · WEB

The word is burden, not sin or failure. Whatever you are carrying qualifies.

In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

Philippians 4:6-7 · WEB

Read the order carefully: prayer, then petition, then thanksgiving, then peace. The peace comes after the asking, and it guards rather than explains.

“Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest.

Matthew 11:28 · WEB

Jesus does not ask the burdened to sort themselves out first. Come is the whole instruction.

A prayer to write

I am carrying more than I can hold tonight. I am not going to list it all, you already know. I am putting it down in front of you because you said I could. Keep it while I sleep.

When the thoughts will not stop

There is a specific kind of anxiety that is not about any one thing. It is the mind running, checking, rehearsing. Two of these verses are about tomorrow, because that is where the running mind lives. The third is about the noise itself.

Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.

Matthew 6:34 · WEB

Not a promise that tomorrow will be fine. A limit on how much of it you are asked to carry today.

In the multitude of my thoughts within me, your comforts delight my soul.

Psalm 94:19 · WEB

The psalmist does not pretend the multitude of thoughts is gone. He says comfort arrived in the middle of them.

You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.

Isaiah 26:3 · WEB

Steadfast does not mean calm. It means pointed in one direction. You can be shaking and still be steadfast.

A prayer to write

My mind will not be quiet. I keep rehearsing things that have not happened. Give me today only, and enough peace for it. Keep my thoughts turned toward you even while they race.

When you are afraid of what is coming

Some anxiety has a date on it. The scan, the meeting, the conversation you have been putting off. These verses are for the days before. They were written to people who were also waiting for something hard.

Don’t you be afraid, for I am with you. Don’t be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you. Yes, I will help you. Yes, I will uphold you with the right hand of my righteousness.

Isaiah 41:10 · WEB

Five promises in one verse: with you, your God, strengthen, help, uphold. Written to a nation in exile, not to people whose lives were going well.

Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you; not as the world gives, I give to you. Don’t let your heart be troubled, neither let it be fearful.

John 14:27 · WEB

Said on the night before the crucifixion, to men who were about to be terrified. Not as the world gives is the point. This peace does not depend on the outcome.

I sought the LORD, and he answered me, and delivered me from all my fears.

Psalm 34:4 · WEB

Past tense. Somebody has already prayed this and had it answered. You are not the first.

A prayer to write

Thursday is coming and I am afraid of it. You know what is in that room before I do. Go ahead of me. Whatever the answer is, be with me when I hear it.

When you need to be still

The last verse is the one most people know, and it is worth reading in full rather than on a mug. It is not a suggestion to relax. It is a command from a God who is going to be exalted regardless, which is exactly why you can stop.

“Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted on the earth.”

Psalm 46:10 · WEB

Be still is the instruction. Know that I am God is the reason. The rest of the verse is the guarantee that the outcome does not rest on you.

A prayer to write

Be still. I am saying it to myself because you said it first. You are God. I am not. Let that be enough for the next ten minutes.

Write it down, then let it come back

Anxious prayers have a particular problem: you forget you prayed them. The thing you were terrified of on a Tuesday in March resolves, or does not, and either way the prayer is gone, and with it the evidence that you were carried through. Six months later the same fear returns and you meet it as if for the first time.

This is the reason to write prayers down rather than only think them. A dated prayer is a record you can be shown again. Prayed does this on purpose: write the prayer, and it brings it back to you at three days, a week, a month and a year, so you can see what you asked for beside what came of it. Nothing you write leaves your phone, which matters more for this kind of prayer than for most.

If you only take one thing from this page, take the habit. Pick one verse above, write the prayer beneath it in your own words, and date it. Then let it come back to you.
