Bible verses for doubt, for when you are not sure you still believe it

Seventeen verses for the week the floor goes, for believing without anything to look at, and for a faith that has got very small. With a short prayer to write for each.

November 9, 20269 min read17 verses, World English Bible

Doubt rarely announces itself. More often you notice it afterwards: you have been saying the words for a few months without anything behind them, or you prayed about something specific and nothing happened, or a question you had parked came back and this time there was no answer waiting. The service carries on around you and you are the only person in the room keeping score.

The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible. A good number of them are spoken by people in exactly that position, and the book does not tidy them up. A father says he believes and asks for help with his unbelief in the same breath. The apostles ask for more faith, which concedes they had been working with a shortage.

They are grouped by where you are rather than by book. If the doubt arrived with months of flatness, no appetite and no sleep, that may be depression rather than an argument about God, and a doctor is part of the answer rather than a detour from it. Read the group that fits this week and leave the other two alone.

The week the floor goes

Something specific usually does it. A prayer that was answered the wrong way, or a person who taught you to pray turning out to be someone else entirely, or a diagnosis. For a while afterwards you cannot tell whether you have stopped believing or are simply very tired, and the two feel identical from inside.

Scripture's first move here is not to argue. It is to let the sentence stand. Every verse in this group is someone reporting a shortage rather than resolving one, and the reporting is addressed to God rather than about him, which is the whole difference.

Immediately the father of the child cried out with tears, “I believe. Help my unbelief!”

Mark 9:24 · WEB

Both halves are true at once and he says them in one sentence, which is permission to stop deciding which one you are. Note that he is asking for help with the unbelief rather than apologising for it.

The apostles said to the Lord, “Increase our faith.”

Luke 17:5 · WEB

The apostles, who had watched everything, ask for an increase. Faith is spoken of here as a quantity that can be short and can be given, not a verdict on your character.

How long, LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?

Psalm 13:1 · WEB

How long is asked twice and the psalm leaves it open for another four verses. Will you forget me forever is an accusation, and it was kept in the book in that form.

My flesh and my heart fails, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.

Psalm 73:26 · WEB

My flesh and my heart fails is stated as a fact and not retracted. What follows does not contradict it; the strength has simply been relocated to somewhere that is not your heart.

Behold, his soul is puffed up. It is not upright in him, but the righteous will live by his faith.

Habakkuk 2:4 · WEB

The first half is about somebody else, a man whose soul is puffed up. The line that gets quoted is the last five words, and live by his faith is offered to a prophet who had spent a chapter complaining that God was doing nothing.

A prayer to write

I do not know whether I still believe this. I am saying it anyway, because saying it to you is the only part I can still manage. I believe. Help my unbelief. Increase whatever is left, because I cannot increase it myself.

When there is nothing to look at

The second kind of doubt is quieter and lasts longer. Nothing has collapsed. You simply want evidence, and the evidence is not coming in the form you want it. People hand you their own experiences, and the gap between a story told to you and a thing that happened to you stays exactly as wide as it was.

These verses do not close that gap. What they do is describe it as the ordinary condition rather than a fault. Hebrews defines faith by the absence of the thing seen. Paul calls walking without sight the normal method. Jesus speaks a blessing over people who will never get what Thomas got.

Now faith is assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen.

Hebrews 11:1 · WEB

Assurance and proof are courtroom words, attached here to things hoped for and things not seen. The definition builds the missing evidence into itself rather than promising to supply it.

for we walk by faith, not by sight.

2 Corinthians 5:7 · WEB

Seven words, and Paul offers them as a description of how this works rather than as a consolation for a bad patch. Not by sight is the standing arrangement.

Jesus said to him, “Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed.”

John 20:29 · WEB

Said to Thomas, who had been given the evidence, and then extended past him to everyone who will not be. If you have ever thought you would believe properly if you had been there, this verse was built for you.

Without faith it is impossible to be well pleasing to him, for he who comes to God must believe that he exists, and that he is a rewarder of those who seek him.

Hebrews 11:6 · WEB

Two things to believe: that he exists, and that he rewards those who seek him. The second is the one most people lose first, and it is listed as half the requirement.

The head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah’s son. If you will not believe, surely you shall not be established.’”

Isaiah 7:9 · WEB

The last line arrives at the end of a political message to a frightened king about two kingdoms that were about to fall. Established is a structural word; the warning is about what holds you up, not about passing a test of sincerity.

A prayer to write

I want something to look at and I am not getting it. Let walking without sight be enough, because you seem to have intended it that way. I am seeking you without much to go on, and you said that gets rewarded. I am holding you to that.

When your faith has got very small

Eventually the question stops being whether you believe and becomes whether this little is worth anything. You can still pray, but thinly, and you suspect the thinness disqualifies it. That suspicion is the thing these last verses go after.

One of them picks the smallest seed in that room and says that quantity is sufficient. Another describes faith arriving by hearing, which makes it something you can be in the way of rather than something you have to generate. Read the pair from James as one sentence, which is how it was written.

He said to them, “Because of your unbelief. For most certainly I tell you, if you have faith as a grain of mustard seed, you will tell this mountain, ‘Move from here to there,’ and it will move; and nothing will be impossible for you.

Matthew 17:20 · WEB

He names unbelief as the problem and then sets the requirement at a mustard seed, which is a strange combination until you notice he is correcting the amount they thought they needed.

But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally and without reproach, and it will be given to him. But let him ask in faith, without any doubting, for he who doubts is like a wave of the sea, driven by the wind and tossed.

James 1:5-6 · WEB

The famous warning about the wave sits immediately after an offer to give wisdom liberally and without reproach. Without reproach is the half that gets dropped, and it means the asking is not held against you.

So faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.

Romans 10:17 · WEB

Comes by hearing makes faith something delivered rather than manufactured. Practically it means putting yourself where the words are said, out loud, on weeks when you can contribute nothing to the room.

Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and don’t lean on your own understanding.

Proverbs 3:5 · WEB

Don't lean on your own understanding is not an instruction to stop thinking. Leaning is what you do with your weight, and the verse is about which of the two things takes it when they disagree.

I am still confident of this: I will see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living. Wait for the LORD. Be strong, and let your heart take courage. Yes, wait for the LORD.

Psalm 27:13-14 · WEB

Still confident, and then wait, be strong, wait again. The repetition is there because the first wait does not hold. In the land of the living means he expected to see it before he died, and he says so without a date.

A prayer to write

This is a very small faith and I am not going to dress it up. You said a seed's worth was enough, so here is mine. Put me where your word is spoken. I am waiting, and I would like to see your goodness before I am done.

Write down what you believed, and when

Doubt does to belief roughly what depression does to memory: it reaches backwards. Once you are inside it, the stretch when you did believe stops looking like evidence and starts looking like a mistake you have now corrected. You cannot argue your way out of that from the inside, because the instrument you would use to argue is the thing in question. What helps is a record, written at the time, with a date on it.
