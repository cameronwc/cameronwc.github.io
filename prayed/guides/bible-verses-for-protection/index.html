Bible verses for protection, for travel and the people you cannot watch

Seventeen verses for the journey you are about to make, the person out there while you are at home, and the weeks when the danger has a name. With a short prayer to write for each.

November 16, 20269 min read17 verses, World English Bible

Nobody has to be talked into praying for safety. It is the prayer that turns up in people who do not otherwise pray: a hand on the roof of the car before a long drive, a sentence said while the plane boards, the thing you say when your son texts that he is leaving now and will be back around midnight. You say it because the alternative is sitting there with nothing.

The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible. It is worth saying first what they do not do, because the disappointment is predictable: none of them promises that nothing will happen. They were kept by people who were hunted, imprisoned and in several cases killed. What they offer is a presence that goes along with you and a place to put yourself, which is a smaller promise than the one most people are hoping for and a more durable one.

They are grouped by where you are standing rather than by book: the journey ahead of you, the person out there while you are at home, and the weeks when the danger has a name. If the danger is a person in your own house, these verses are not a substitute for the police or a domestic abuse line, and calling one is not a failure of faith.

Before a journey, and during it

There is a particular helplessness in the hours of a trip. You did the sensible things, the tyres and the hotel and leaving early, and then they run out and there are five hundred miles left that you cannot do anything about except drive them. Praying in that gap is not an attempt to steer. It is a way of not spending the trip alone with the odds.

These are travel verses in the plain sense. Going out and coming in, all your ways, wherever you go, the uttermost parts of the sea: the language is roads and distance, and it keeps naming both ends of the trip.

The LORD will keep you from all evil. He will keep your soul. The LORD will keep your going out and your coming in, from this time forward, and forever more.

Psalm 121:7-8 · WEB

Keep is said three times in two verses, and the last one covers the going out and the coming in, which is a traveller's way of saying the whole of it.

If I take the wings of the dawn, and settle in the uttermost parts of the sea, even there your hand will lead me, and your right hand will hold me.

Psalm 139:9-10 · WEB

He picks the two furthest places he can think of, and the sentence does not end in a rescue. It ends in a hand that leads and a hand that holds, which is what you want during a journey rather than after it.

Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.”

Joshua 1:9 · WEB

Said to a man about to take a nation across a river into a war he had not chosen. The order is to stop being afraid, not to stop going.

For he will put his angels in charge of you, to guard you in all your ways.

Psalm 91:11 · WEB

In all your ways, and ways are roads. Put his angels in charge of you is an instruction given to someone else about you; your part is to be the one travelling.

The LORD’s angel encamps around those who fear him, and delivers them.

Psalm 34:7 · WEB

Encamps is a word from the road. A camp goes up wherever you stopped for the night, so the picture is of something arriving where you are rather than a place you have to reach.

A prayer to write

I am about to get in the car and I have done what I know how to do. Keep my going out and my coming in. Go the whole way with me rather than meeting me at the other end.

When the person out there is not you

This is the harder one. On your own trip you can at least watch the road. When it is your daughter's first long drive, or a flight you are not on, or someone walking home from a late shift, there is nothing for your attention to do, so it goes round and round until the door opens.

These verses move the safekeeping somewhere other than your watching, which is their use at one in the morning. Two of them are not requests at all. They are sentences said out loud about God, in the first person, and saying a thing out loud hands it over in a way that lying there quietly does not.

But the Lord is faithful, who will establish you and guard you from the evil one.

2 Thessalonians 3:3 · WEB

The faithfulness in the sentence is his, not yours and not theirs. Establish and guard are ongoing words, the sort of thing done to a wall over years rather than once.

He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High will rest in the shadow of the Almighty. I will say of the LORD, “He is my refuge and my fortress; my God, in whom I trust.”

Psalm 91:1-2 · WEB

Dwells, not visits. Then notice the switch: the second verse asks for nothing, it is the writer telling himself what is true, out loud. I will say is something you can do at one in the morning.

You are my hiding place. You will preserve me from trouble. You will surround me with songs of deliverance. Selah.

Psalm 32:7 · WEB

A hiding place is for the person being looked for. What surrounds him is not a wall but singing about rescue, which implies the rescue has a history.

In peace I will both lay myself down and sleep, for you alone, LORD, make me live in safety.

Psalm 4:8 · WEB

The reason given for sleeping is not that the risk has passed, and alone is the word doing the work. For the parent still awake, this is the verse, and it is short enough to say in the dark.

A prayer to write

They are out there, I am here, and there is nothing for me to do. You are the one who keeps them, not my staying awake. Guard them tonight, and let me sleep.

When the danger has a name

Sometimes it is not a trip. There is a person who frightens you, or a court date, or a street you walk down twice a day, or a war on the news with people you know inside it. Praying changes character here. It gets shorter, and it stops asking for a good outcome in general and starts asking for one thing.

Read the verse from Isaiah to the end before you use it. No weapon that is formed against you will prevail gets quoted on its own constantly, and the rest of the sentence is about tongues that rise in judgment and a heritage belonging to the Lord's servants. Using it as a charm sets you up to decide afterwards that you did it wrong.

The LORD’s name is a strong tower: the righteous run to him, and are safe.

Proverbs 18:10 · WEB

Run to him is a verb, and running is done with the body. The tower is already standing, so your part is covering the distance to it.

The LORD is my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my rock, in whom I take refuge; my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my high tower.

Psalm 18:2 · WEB

Six names for one thing in a single breath. The piling up is not decoration; it is what people do when they cannot get something said in one word.

According to Alamoth. God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.

Psalm 46:1 · WEB

In trouble, not after it. The opening phrase is the psalm's own musical heading, carried in by the translation. Present is a claim about distance rather than strength.

No weapon that is formed against you will prevail; and you will condemn every tongue that rises against you in judgment. This is the heritage of the LORD’s servants, and their righteousness is of me,” says the LORD.

Isaiah 54:17 · WEB

Here is the whole sentence. Judgment is the setting, the tongues are listed alongside the weapons, and the last clause assigns the righteousness to God rather than to you. Heritage means handed down.

Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid or scared of them, for the LORD your God himself is who goes with you. He will not fail you nor forsake you.”

Deuteronomy 31:6 · WEB

The grammar is emphatic: himself is who goes with you. Said to a people about to face armies, so the courage is commanded with full knowledge of what is coming.

A prayer to write

I am not asking in general. I am asking about this, by name, and I would like it to go a particular way. You are a strong tower and I am running to you instead of standing here.

Write down what you asked, and the date

Prayers for safety are the ones you forget fastest. You say one in a car park, the trip happens, nothing comes of the nothing that happened, and there is never an occasion to look back at it. After a few years of praying this way the only episode you can remember is the one time it went badly, which is a poor sample, and it quietly teaches you that the praying made no difference.
