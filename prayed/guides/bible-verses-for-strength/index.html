Bible verses for strength, for when you have run out of your own

Seventeen verses for the end of your reserves, for the long carry, and for where the strength is actually coming from. With a short prayer to write for each.

September 28, 20269 min read17 verses, World English Bible

Strength is the wrong word for what most people are after when they go looking for verses about it. Nobody types it into a search box wanting to be stronger in general. They want to get through Thursday, or through the next six months of caring for someone, or through a job they cannot leave yet. The request is nearly always specific, and it is usually made by someone already running on very little.

The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible and grouped by where you are rather than by book. Read what they actually claim. Most of them do not say you will find reserves you did not know you had. They say the opposite: the strength belongs to someone else and is being lent, and the qualification for receiving it is having none of your own.

One plain thing first. Tiredness that does not lift after rest is not always a spiritual problem, and these verses are not a substitute for a doctor. Exhaustion is a symptom of a long list of ordinary treatable things, and of depression, and it is worth saying out loud to someone who can test for them. Read the group that matches this week and leave the rest.

When there is none left

This is the end of the reserves. Not a hard day, which you can do, but the point after a run of them where the ordinary things have started costing what the hard things used to cost. You are still functioning, probably. You are just doing it on nothing.

Scripture is unusually blunt here. These verses do not tell you that you are stronger than you think. Three of them begin by agreeing that you have run out, and then say what happens after that.

My flesh and my heart fails, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.

Psalm 73:26 · WEB

The failing is in the present tense and it is not walked back. The psalm concedes the whole first clause and puts something behind it, rather than arguing you out of it.

He gives power to the weak. He increases the strength of him who has no might.

Isaiah 40:29 · WEB

The qualification is having no might. Written to people who had been in exile long enough to stop expecting anything.

He has said to me, “My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.” Most gladly therefore I will rather glory in my weaknesses, that the power of Christ may rest on me.

2 Corinthians 12:9 · WEB

Paul had asked three times for the thing to be taken away, and this is the answer he got instead. Made perfect in weakness means the weakness is where the power works, not the obstacle it works around.

In the day that I called, you answered me. You encouraged me with strength in my soul.

Psalm 138:3 · WEB

The answer came the same day, and what arrived was strength in his soul rather than a change to the situation he was calling about.

According to Alamoth. God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.

Psalm 46:1 · WEB

In trouble, not after it. The opening phrase is the psalm's own musical heading, carried in by the translation. A very present help is the part to sit on: the claim is about proximity.

A prayer to write

I have nothing left and I am tired of pretending otherwise. My heart is failing. You say you give power to people with no might, so I am asking as one of those, today and not later, for enough to get through what is actually in front of me.

When you have to keep going anyway

The harder case is not the day you collapse. It is the stretch where collapsing is not available. Someone depends on you, the treatment runs another eight months, the job has to be kept until something else comes up. You are not asking to be rescued from it. You are asking to carry it without breaking, which is a smaller prayer and a different one.

These verses are about the long carry, and they are worth reading for their pace. Almost none of them promise speed, and one offers rest and then hands you something to carry.

“Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; and you will find rest for your souls.

Matthew 11:28-29 · WEB

He says rest and then immediately says yoke, which reads oddly. A yoke is not a way of stopping. It is a way of pulling a load that spreads the weight: the offer is to be put in one with him, not let out of the field.

but those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.

Isaiah 40:31 · WEB

Mount up, run, walk. The list gets less impressive as it goes, and the last item is the one most people need. Renew assumes waiting first, which is the part nobody wants.

Therefore we don’t faint, but though our outward person is decaying, yet our inward person is renewed day by day.

2 Corinthians 4:16 · WEB

Paul grants the decay of the outward person without softening it. Day by day is a rate rather than a total, which is the honest scale for a long stretch.

I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.

Philippians 4:13 · WEB

The most quoted verse about strength and the most often bent. Two verses earlier Paul says what all things means: he has learned to be content hungry or full. It is about enduring circumstances, not achieving what you have decided to achieve.

Finally, be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might.

Ephesians 6:10 · WEB

The command is to be strong, and both halves of the sentence then place the strength outside you. It opens the passage about armour, all of which is worn rather than generated.

A prayer to write

I am not asking to be let out of this. I am asking to be able to carry it. Renew what I have day by day, at whatever rate you do that, and let walking without fainting count as an answer, because it would be one.

Where the strength is actually coming from

The verses in this last group are the ones people skip, because they look like praise rather than help. Between them they name where the strength is kept, and the answer is never inside the person praying.

Two of them are songs, and one was written down as a song deliberately, by a man who had just been told the invasion was coming and would not be called off. That is the shape of most of these: not relief, but something to stand on while the thing you feared happens anyway.

The LORD is my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my rock, in whom I take refuge; my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my high tower.

Psalm 18:2 · WEB

Seven images in one sentence: rock, fortress, deliverer, refuge, shield, horn, high tower. Every one is something you stand on or shelter behind. None of them is something you turn into.

GOD, the Lord, is my strength. He makes my feet like deer’s feet, and enables me to go in high places. For the music director, on my stringed instruments.

Habakkuk 3:19 · WEB

Habakkuk wrote this at the end of a book about an invasion God had confirmed was coming. Deer's feet are for staying upright on ground that is steep and loose. The last line is his instruction to the musicians.

Then he said to them, “Go your way. Eat the fat, drink the sweet, and send portions to him for whom nothing is prepared, for today is holy to our Lord. Don’t be grieved, for the joy of the LORD is your strength.”

Nehemiah 8:10 · WEB

The crowd was weeping as the law was read to them, and this is the response: eat, drink, and send a portion to someone who has nothing prepared. The joy is described as the source rather than the result.

The LORD is my strength and my shield. My heart has trusted in him, and I am helped. Therefore my heart greatly rejoices. With my song I will thank him.

Psalm 28:7 · WEB

Watch the order. Trusted, then helped, then the heart rejoices, then the song. The gladness is downstream of being helped and is not asked for first.

The LORD is my strength and song. He has become my salvation. This is my God, and I will praise him; my father’s God, and I will exalt him.

Exodus 15:2 · WEB

Sung on the far side of the water, looking back at it. Strength and song arrive in the same breath, which is the pattern across this whole group.

Behold, God is my salvation. I will trust, and will not be afraid; for the LORD, the LORD, is my strength and song; and he has become my salvation.”

Isaiah 12:2 · WEB

Isaiah is quoting the song from Exodus, centuries later, to a different people facing a different threat. That is what these last two verses recommend: when your own words run out, sing an old one about a rescue you did not see.

A prayer to write

I have been trying to be the strong one and it is not a thing I can be. You are the rock and I am not. Be my strength today in the plain sense of the word, and if a song is part of how that arrives, teach me one, because I do not have one at the moment.

Write down the weeks you got through, and date them

Strength is almost impossible to assess from inside a hard stretch. The weeks you did get through leave no mark, so they stop counting, and the impression that you have been failing the whole time goes unchallenged because there is nothing to check it against. A dated record is the plain correction.
