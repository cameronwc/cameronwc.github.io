Bible verses for sleep and rest, for the nights you cannot switch off

Seventeen verses for the nights you lie awake, the stretch where you are too tired to stop, and a peace that does not wait for things to calm down. With a short prayer to write for each.

October 12, 202610 min read17 verses, World English Bible

Rest is a strange thing to need help with. Nobody had to teach you how to sleep, and yet here you are at one in the morning with tomorrow already loaded, running the same three problems in the same order, doing arithmetic about how many hours are left. The arithmetic is itself a way of staying awake. Or the opposite: you are sleeping the whole night and waking up tired anyway, which is its own puzzle and does not respond to going to bed earlier.

The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible and grouped by three different versions of the problem rather than by book: the night you cannot get to sleep, the stretch where there is no gap to put rest into, and the mind that will not go quiet whatever your circumstances are doing. Read what they actually say. Several of them move sleep out of the column of things you accomplish and into the column of things handed to you, which is a different claim from the one most advice about sleep is making.

None of this replaces a doctor. Weeks of not sleeping, or tiredness that a full night does not touch, is a medical question before it is a spiritual one and worth saying out loud to someone who can test for it. Read the group that matches the night you are having and leave the other two for another week.

The nights you cannot get to sleep

The particular misery of this one is that effort makes it worse. Sleep is the only task on the list that is undone by trying harder at it, so there is nothing to do but lie there while your mind helpfully produces every unresolved thing from the last six months in no useful order. By three in the morning the problems have grown to a size they will not have at breakfast, which you know, and knowing it does not shrink them.

The verses here do not offer a technique and none of them promises eight hours. What they do is put the giving of sleep somewhere other than in your hands, and one of them says out loud that the late nights are vain, in the voice of somebody who had tried a few.

In peace I will both lay myself down and sleep, for you alone, LORD, make me live in safety.

Psalm 4:8 · WEB

Lay myself down and sleep are counted as two separate things, which anyone who has done the first without the second will recognise. The reason given is not a calm mind but safety, and it is placed outside him.

It is vain for you to rise up early, to stay up late, eating the bread of toil, for he gives sleep to his loved ones.

Psalm 127:2 · WEB

Vain is aimed at the early rising and the late nights, not at the work itself. Bread of toil is the detail to sit with. Sleep then appears as something he gives, with no method attached.

In the multitude of my thoughts within me, your comforts delight my soul.

Psalm 94:19 · WEB

The multitude of my thoughts within me is the two in the morning problem described exactly, three thousand years early. Note that the comforts are plural and that they do not clear the thoughts out, they arrive among them.

He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High will rest in the shadow of the Almighty.

Psalm 91:1 · WEB

Dwells, not visits. And the rest is located in a shadow, which only matters to someone who is out in the heat, so the verse assumes the exposure rather than denying it.

Return to your rest, my soul, for the LORD has dealt bountifully with you.

Psalm 116:7 · WEB

He is talking to his own soul, which is what you end up doing at that hour. Return admits he had left, and the ground he gives is in the past tense: what has already been done, not what he expects tonight.

A prayer to write

It is late and I am still awake and I have run out of ways to make this happen. None of it is getting solved tonight. You give sleep to the people you love, and I am asking for that, and for my thoughts to be quiet enough to take it.

When you are too tired to stop

A different problem, and the more common one: not that rest will not come but that there is no gap to put it in. The work does not finish. The children do not stop needing things. An afternoon off is paid for with the evening, so you keep going, and after long enough the tiredness stops being an event you are recovering from and becomes the weather you live in.

These verses are addressed to people in exactly that position, several of them to people in the middle of a job. Nothing in them waits for the demand to let up first; in the passage from Mark the crowd is still arriving. And look at what is actually offered, because in two of them the rest comes attached to a load or a journey rather than instead of one.

“Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; and you will find rest for your souls.

Matthew 11:28-29 · WEB

Rest is given in the first sentence and found in the second, and between them he hands over a yoke, which is farm equipment for pulling things. The offer is not an empty afternoon. It is a different weight and a gentler person to carry it with.

He said to them, “Come away into a deserted place, and rest awhile.” For there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.

Mark 6:31 · WEB

He tells them to stop in the middle of the demand, not after it. Awhile is a modest and specific amount of time. The reason given is that they had not managed to eat, which is the kind of detail that gets noticed by someone who is watching rather than managing.

For thus said the Lord GOD, the Holy One of Israel, “You will be saved in returning and rest. Your strength will be in quietness and in confidence.” You refused,

Isaiah 30:15 · WEB

Returning, rest, quietness and confidence, offered in one sentence, and then the verse breaks off on You refused. Both halves are scripture. It is worth reading the refusal as well as the offer, because declining rest is a thing people do on purpose.

He said, “My presence will go with you, and I will give you rest.”

Exodus 33:14 · WEB

Said to a man with a journey still ahead of him and no arrival in sight. The presence goes with you and the rest comes with the presence, which means it is not being held back until the end of the trip.

The LORD is my shepherd; I shall lack nothing. He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters.

Psalm 23:1-2 · WEB

He makes me lie down. The lying down is caused rather than chosen, which is the honest order of events for anyone who will not stop voluntarily. Still waters, in a job that involved moving animals all day.

A prayer to write

I am tired in a way that sleep has not fixed and I cannot see where the stopping is supposed to go. Make me lie down, because I will not do it on my own. Let your presence come with me into this week rather than waiting for the end of it.

When your mind will not go quiet

The third version does not care what your circumstances are. The bills are paid, nothing is wrong this week, the house is dark and quiet, and the low hum continues anyway. Get this straight before you start asking: peace in these verses is not defined as the absence of trouble. If that is what you are asking for, you will decide the verses are not working, when what has happened is that they promised something else.

The one about being still is the most quoted and the least read. It turns up on its own, in a good font, over a photograph of a lake. The two sentences that follow it in the same verse are about nations, and the psalm it sits in opens with the earth giving way and the mountains falling into the sea.

Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you; not as the world gives, I give to you. Don’t let your heart be troubled, neither let it be fearful.

John 14:27 · WEB

Said in the last few hours before his arrest, which is the context that makes not as the world gives mean something. The world's version requires the conditions to be favourable. This one is handed over on the worst night of the week.

And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

Philippians 4:7 · WEB

Guard is a soldier's word, and what it guards is your hearts and your thoughts, which is precisely the part that will not switch off. Surpasses all understanding also means you will not be able to account for it, so do not wait until it makes sense.

You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.

Isaiah 26:3 · WEB

The keeping is done to you, not by you. The condition attached is trust rather than calm, which is a relief, because calm is the thing you do not have at the moment you need this verse.

“Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted on the earth.”

Psalm 46:10 · WEB

Be still, and know. The stillness is not an end in itself; it is there so that something can be known, and the two sentences after it are about the whole earth rather than about your evening.

My soul rests in God alone. My salvation is from him.

Psalm 62:1 · WEB

Alone is the word doing the work in both sentences. He is not resting in God and also in a plan, a savings account and an early night. The narrowing is the point.

A prayer to write

Nothing is wrong and I still cannot settle. I am not asking for everything to calm down first. Guard my thoughts the way you said you would, and keep me, because steadfast is not something I can hold on to by myself tonight.

Write it down at night, and date it

Bad nights are almost impossible to remember accurately. A rough fortnight and a rough year feel identical at two in the morning, and by breakfast you are too tired to take notes on either. So the one thing that would tell you whether this is a season or a pattern, a dated record of the nights, is the thing nobody keeps. Instead you carry around a vague sense of not having slept properly in a while, which is no use to you and no use to a doctor.
