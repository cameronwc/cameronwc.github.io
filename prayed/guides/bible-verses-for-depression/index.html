Bible verses for depression, for the mornings you cannot get up

Fifteen verses for the worst mornings, the long grey middle, and the days a little of it lifts. With a short prayer to write for each.

September 21, 20269 min read19 verses, World English Bible

Depression is not a bad mood that has gone on too long. It is closer to a flatness: the things that used to matter look the same from the outside and produce nothing on the inside, and getting out of bed turns into a decision you have to make several times before it takes. People who have not had it tend to arrive with reasons to cheer up, and reasons are precisely the thing that has stopped working.

The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible. Several were written by people who were in it: a psalmist asking how long, a prophet promising something he could not yet see, an apostle listing the things that had not quite finished him off. They are grouped by where you are rather than by book, because the verse that is useless on the worst morning is often the one that carries the sixth month.

None of this is a substitute for a doctor or a crisis line. If you have stopped eating or sleeping, or you have thought about not being here, tell someone today who can do something about it; these verses are for alongside that, not instead of it. Read the group that matches where you are and leave the rest.

The mornings you cannot get up

This is the acute part. The alarm goes, and the day in front of you is a list of things you cannot see yourself doing, not one of which is actually difficult. That is the strange cruelty of it. The difficulty is not in the tasks.

Scripture does not meet that with encouragement. It meets it first by putting the complaint on the record. The verse at the top of this group is a question with no answer attached, and it was left in the book that way.

How long, LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?

Psalm 13:1 · WEB

How long is asked twice, and the psalm does not resolve it for another four verses. If this is all you can manage this morning, you are praying a psalm.

Hurry to answer me, LORD. My spirit fails. Don’t hide your face from me, so that I don’t become like those who go down into the pit.

Psalm 143:7 · WEB

My spirit fails is a plain report, not a confession. Notice what he asks for: speed, and a face that is not turned away.

Why are you in despair, my soul? Why are you disturbed within me? Hope in God! For I shall still praise him, the saving help of my countenance, and my God.

Psalm 42:11 · WEB

The psalmist is talking to himself, which is what you end up doing. He does not tell his soul to feel better. He tells it where to put its hope, and says he shall still praise him. Still, later, not now.

The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit.

Psalm 34:18 · WEB

Near to the broken, saves the crushed. Both are descriptions of a person rather than of a mood, and nothing is asked of them first.

But you, LORD, are a shield around me, my glory, and the one who lifts up my head.

Psalm 3:3 · WEB

The one who lifts up my head. David wrote it while running from his own son. A head that will not lift is a physical fact before it is a spiritual one, and this verse takes it that way.

A prayer to write

I cannot do today and I am not going to pretend otherwise. My spirit fails. Be near, the way you said you are near to people whose hearts are broken, and lift my head, because I cannot lift it myself.

The long grey middle

After the worst of it, and sometimes for years, there is a version that is not dramatic at all. You go to work. You answer the messages. Nothing is happening and nothing is improving, and the effort of holding that shape is invisible to everyone, including, most days, you.

These verses are about endurance rather than rescue. Read what they actually promise. Not that the pressure stops, but a list of the things it does not get to do.

We are pressed on every side, yet not crushed; perplexed, yet not to despair; pursued, yet not forsaken; struck down, yet not destroyed;

2 Corinthians 4:8-9 · WEB

Four pairs, and Paul concedes the first half of every one of them. Pressed, perplexed, pursued, struck down. He argues with none of it. The word doing the work is yet.

My soul, wait in silence for God alone, for my expectation is from him.

Psalm 62:5 · WEB

Wait in silence is not the same as feeling nothing. It is a decision to stop arguing with yourself for a while, and the expectation is placed outside you, where you cannot drain it.

For his anger is but for a moment. His favor is for a lifetime. Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning.

Psalm 30:5 · WEB

Weeping may stay for the night. The psalm does not say how long the night is, and read as a schedule this verse will hurt you. Read it as a direction instead.

but those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.

Isaiah 40:31 · WEB

Mount up, run, walk. The order matters, because walking and not fainting comes last and is the one most people actually need. It was written to people already worn out by a long exile.

to provide for those who mourn in Zion, to give to them a garland for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness, that they may be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the LORD, that he may be glorified.

Isaiah 61:3 · WEB

A garland for ashes, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness. Heaviness is the right word for this. The verse describes an exchange, not a denial: the ashes were real and they are what gets traded.

A prayer to write

Nothing is wrong today and I still cannot feel anything. I am not asking for the pressure to stop this week. Keep me from being crushed and keep me from being destroyed, and let walking without fainting be enough for now.

When a little of it lifts

Coming out of depression is rarely an event. It is a morning that is slightly less heavy than the one before it, noticed only afterwards, and usually distrusted when it arrives. These verses are for that: small evidence, taken seriously.

Two of them describe the lifting as slow and deliberate, and one of them is an act of memory performed on purpose. That is worth noticing, because memory is the part depression takes first.

This I recall to my mind; therefore I have hope. It is because of the LORD’s loving kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his mercies don’t fail. They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:21-23 · WEB

Recall to my mind is something he does deliberately, in the middle of a book that is otherwise all lament, and the hope follows the recalling rather than the other way round. New every morning is the line to hold.

I waited patiently for the LORD. He turned to me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay. He set my feet on a rock, and gave me a firm place to stand.

Psalm 40:1-2 · WEB

Waited patiently comes before the pit, the clay, the rock and the firm place. Four steps in that order, and not one of them is instant.

Cause me to hear your loving kindness in the morning, for I trust in you. Cause me to know the way in which I should walk, for I lift up my soul to you.

Psalm 143:8 · WEB

Two requests for one morning: to hear that he is loved, and to be told where to walk. On a slightly better day that is a usable size of ask.

But I trust in your loving kindness. My heart rejoices in your salvation.

Psalm 13:5 · WEB

Same psalm as the how long at the top of this page. The turn is real and the complaint has not been deleted. Both are on the page, which is permission to have both.

Now may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that you may abound in hope in the power of the Holy Spirit.

Romans 15:13 · WEB

Fill, abound, power. The language is oversized on purpose, and the hope is something given rather than generated. If it does not feel true yet, it is still a thing you are allowed to ask for.

A prayer to write

Something was a little lighter this week and I am afraid to trust it. Let me recall it on purpose instead of losing it. Set my feet on something that holds. Fill me with hope I did not have to manufacture.

Write it down on the bad days, and date it

Depression lies about time, and it lies most about the past. It tells you that you have always felt this way, and that the better weeks either did not happen or did not count. Memory is the first thing it takes, which is why the man in Lamentations has to recall on purpose. A dated record is the plain correction to that. It is not an argument, just evidence with a date attached.
