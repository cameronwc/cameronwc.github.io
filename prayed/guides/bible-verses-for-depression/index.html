Bible verses for depression, for the days you cannot feel anything

Eighteen verses for the bottom, for the long grind in the middle, and for the morning something begins to lift. With a short prayer to write for each.

September 14, 20269 min read18 verses, World English Bible

Depression is not the same thing as sadness, and the difference matters when you go looking for verses. Sadness has an object. You are sad about something, and the something can usually be named. Depression often has nothing attached to it at all. It is flat rather than sharp, it arrives without a reason and stays long after the reason is gone, and its worst feature is not pain but the absence of feeling: you can be told good news and register none of it.

The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible, and they are grouped by where you are rather than by book. The bottom, where getting through the day is the whole of the work. The long middle, where you are functioning and nothing is moving. And the point where something begins to lift, which is harder to trust than people expect. A good many of these verses are complaints. Scripture holds far more of those than most collections of encouraging verses admit, and they are useful precisely because they do not ask you to feel better first.

None of this is a substitute for a doctor, a therapist or a crisis line. If you have been thinking about hurting yourself, call one today; the verses will still be here afterwards. Depression is an illness as well as a weight, and treating it as only one of those has cost people a great deal. Read the group that matches this week and leave the rest.

When you are at the bottom

This is the week you cannot get out of bed, or you get out of bed and it takes everything you have. Nothing is interesting. Food is a chore. The people who love you feel like work, and knowing that they do makes it worse. You are not going to be argued out of this, and none of the verses here attempt it.

What they offer instead is permission to say it. Three of the five below are complaints made directly to God, which means the complaint has been a normal part of prayer for about three thousand years and does not need tidying up before you make it.

Why are you in despair, my soul? Why are you disturbed within me? Hope in God! For I shall still praise him, the saving help of my countenance, and my God.

Psalm 42:11 · WEB

The psalmist talks to his own soul, asks it twice what is wrong, and gets no answer. Then he tells it to hope anyway. Notice the order: the question is not settled before the hope, and the psalm ends without the mood lifting.

Hurry to answer me, LORD. My spirit fails. Don’t hide your face from me, so that I don’t become like those who go down into the pit. Cause me to hear your loving kindness in the morning, for I trust in you. Cause me to know the way in which I should walk, for I lift up my soul to you.

Psalm 143:7-8 · WEB

My spirit fails is not an exaggeration and David does not soften it. What he asks for next is small and specific, one kind thing heard in the morning, which is often the right size of prayer at the bottom.

The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit.

Psalm 34:18 · WEB

Near to the broken-hearted, saves the crushed in spirit. Crushed is the accurate word for this, and it is the verse's own word, not a diagnosis you have to justify to anyone.

We are pressed on every side, yet not crushed; perplexed, yet not to despair; pursued, yet not forsaken; struck down, yet not destroyed;

2 Corinthians 4:8-9 · WEB

Paul writes four pairs and concedes the first half of every one. Pressed, perplexed, pursued, struck down are all allowed to stand as true. Only the second half of each is denied.

But I trust in your loving kindness. My heart rejoices in your salvation.

Psalm 13:5 · WEB

The six verses before this one are almost entirely how long. But is carrying the whole line. You can pray this without feeling it, which is the only way it ever gets prayed.

A prayer to write

I cannot feel anything and I have stopped pretending otherwise. My spirit fails. I am not asking to be happy this week. Be near in the way you said you are near to people like this, and let me hear one kind thing in the morning.

When it has gone on a long time

The second phase is the one nobody warns you about. Not the crisis, the grind. Months in, you are functioning. You go to work, you answer messages, you are on paper fine, and nothing has moved. This is where people quietly stop praying, because asking for the same thing in the ninth month feels absurd, and because it is easier to stop than to keep hearing your own voice ask.

These verses are for the ninth month. Two of them are memory exercises rather than promises, which sounds like a thin offer until you notice that memory is the first thing this illness takes.

He brought me up also out of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay. He set my feet on a rock, and gave me a firm place to stand.

Psalm 40:2 · WEB

Brought me up, set my feet, gave me a firm place. Three verbs, all past tense. This is a verse to say about a previous time you were down, on a day when you can say nothing at all about this one.

This I recall to my mind; therefore I have hope. It is because of the LORD’s loving kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his mercies don’t fail. They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:21-23 · WEB

Jeremiah is not feeling it either; the lines just before these are among the bleakest in the Bible. This I recall to my mind is a deliberate act, done against the evidence. New every morning is not a claim that the morning will feel new.

But you, LORD, are a shield around me, my glory, and the one who lifts up my head.

Psalm 3:3 · WEB

Lifts up my head is a physical description, and depression is physical. Some days this is the only thing you can think to ask for, and it is enough to ask for.

“Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest.

Matthew 11:28 · WEB

Heavily burdened is the invitation's only condition. What is offered is rest rather than a task, and there is nothing in the sentence to do first.

My soul, wait in silence for God alone, for my expectation is from him.

Psalm 62:5 · WEB

Wait in silence is permission to have nothing to say. The expectation at the end of the verse is not yours to produce.

A prayer to write

Nothing has changed and I am tired of asking. I am writing it down again anyway. I remember that you brought me up before, even though I cannot remember what that felt like. Lift my head. I have nothing to say after that.

When something starts to lift

Recovery from depression is rarely announced. It usually turns up as a small thing you only notice a week later. You wanted a particular meal. You rang someone back. A piece of music landed instead of passing through. The difficulty at this stage is a new one: you are afraid to trust it, because you have been here before and it went again.

These verses do not promise it will not go again. They describe an exchange and a renewal, and every one of them is careful about how long such things take.

For his anger is but for a moment. His favor is for a lifetime. Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning.

Psalm 30:5 · WEB

Weeping may stay for the night. The psalmist never says how long the night is, and these nights are long. The verse is about direction rather than schedule.

but those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.

Isaiah 40:31 · WEB

Read the order of the verbs: mount up, run, walk. It ends at walking, which is the slowest of the three and the last thing to come back, and walking is what the verse promises will not fail.

to provide for those who mourn in Zion, to give to them a garland for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness, that they may be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the LORD, that he may be glorified.

Isaiah 61:3 · WEB

An exchange, not a denial. Garland for ashes, oil for mourning, praise for the spirit of heaviness. Heaviness is exactly the right word, and here it is being swapped rather than argued with.

Now may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that you may abound in hope in the power of the Holy Spirit.

Romans 15:13 · WEB

Fill and abound are large words for a small week. Note that Paul is praying this for other people, which is what to do with the verse when you cannot pray it for yourself: send it to someone and let them pray it over you.

A prayer to write

Something is a little lighter this week and I am afraid to say so in case saying it ends it. Thank you for it anyway. Renew what has been worn out, at whatever pace it comes, and keep me walking on the days that is all there is.

Write it down on the bad days, and date it

Depression lies about time, and it lies in one direction. It tells you that things have always been this bad and always will be. You cannot reason with that in the moment, because the evidence it would take, what last March was actually like, what you wrote in June, is exactly what the illness has taken from you.

A dated record is the correction. Prayed keeps one.
