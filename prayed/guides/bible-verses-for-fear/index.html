Bible verses for fear, for the night before

Twelve verses for when you are frightened of something specific, sorted by whether you need to be told you are not alone, told to move, or told whose you are.

September 11, 20266 min read12 verses, World English Bible

Fear is more honest than anxiety. It usually knows what it is afraid of. The surgery is at seven. The court date is Monday. The test comes back this week. That specificity makes fear easier to pray about and harder to pray away, because the thing really is coming.

The Bible says do not be afraid more often than it says almost anything else, and it never says it to people who have nothing to fear. It says it to a man about to lead an army across a river, to exiles, to a girl who has just been told she will bear a child. The verses below are quoted word for word from the World English Bible and grouped by the three things a frightened person needs to hear: you are not alone, go anyway, and you are mine.

Each group ends with a prayer you could write down tonight, dated, so that you can find it again after the thing has happened and see what you were given.

You are not alone

Nearly every do not be afraid in scripture is followed by the same reason, and it is never that the danger is smaller than you think. It is presence. These four are the clearest.

Don’t you be afraid, for I am with you. Don’t be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you. Yes, I will help you. Yes, I will uphold you with the right hand of my righteousness.

Isaiah 41:10 · WEB

The verse most people memorize for fear, and for good reason. Five promises, one condition: none.

The LORD himself is who goes before you. He will be with you. He will not fail you nor forsake you. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be discouraged.”

Deuteronomy 31:8 · WEB

Said to Joshua as Moses hands over the job. Goes before you is the part to hold onto. You are not walking into the room first.

Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me. Your rod and your staff, they comfort me.

Psalm 23:4 · WEB

The valley is not skipped. It is walked through. The comfort is company, not a detour.

He will cover you with his feathers. Under his wings you will take refuge. His faithfulness is your shield and rampart.

Psalm 91:4 · WEB

The image is a bird covering its young. Not a fortress, something warmer and closer than that.

A prayer to write

I am afraid of tomorrow and I do not want to pretend otherwise. You said you go before me. Go, then. Be in the room before I get there, and stay after I leave.

Go anyway

Some fear needs comfort. Some needs a push. These are the verses for the fear that has started to make decisions for you, the kind that keeps you from the phone call, the appointment, the conversation.

Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.”

Joshua 1:9 · WEB

It is a command, not a suggestion, and it is given to someone who has every reason to be afraid. Courage here is a thing you are told to do, not a thing you wait to feel.

For God didn’t give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control.

2 Timothy 1:7 · WEB

Paul writing from prison to a younger man who was losing his nerve. Fear is named as a spirit, and it is not the one you were given.

When I am afraid, I will put my trust in you.

Psalm 56:3 · WEB

The shortest and most honest verse on this page. When, not if. Fear and trust in the same breath.

The LORD is on my side. I will not be afraid. What can man do to me?

Psalm 118:6 · WEB

A rhetorical question, and the psalmist knows the real answer is quite a lot. He asks it anyway.

A prayer to write

I have been letting fear choose for me. I am going to make the call today. Give me the courage you commanded, not the courage I feel, and let that be enough.

You are mine

The deepest answer to fear in scripture is not a promise about the outcome. It is a statement about who you belong to. These four verses say it in four different ways.

But now the LORD who created you, Jacob, and he who formed you, Israel, says: “Don’t be afraid, for I have redeemed you. I have called you by your name. You are mine.

Isaiah 43:1 · WEB

Called by your name. You are mine. The verse right after this one is about passing through waters and fire. Belonging comes first.

For you didn’t receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry, “Abba! Father!”

Romans 8:15 · WEB

Abba is the word a small child uses. The opposite of fear, here, is not bravery. It is being someone's.

The LORD is my light and my salvation. Whom shall I fear? The LORD is the strength of my life. Of whom shall I be afraid?

Psalm 27:1 · WEB

Two questions with the same answer. If the LORD is your light, there is no one left on the list.

There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear, because fear has punishment. He who fears is not made perfect in love.

1 John 4:18 · WEB

The hardest verse in this group, and worth sitting with. It does not say fear is wrong. It says love, when it is finished with you, will not leave room for it.

A prayer to write

Before the outcome, before the answer, I am yours. Say my name over me tonight. Let that settle something that the news tomorrow cannot unsettle.

Date the prayer, then come back to it

Fear has a short memory. The thing you dreaded happens or does not, and either way the dread evaporates, taking with it any record that you were carried. The next fear arrives and you face it as though you have never been through one.

Writing the prayer down, with a date, fixes that.
